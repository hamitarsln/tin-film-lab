# Brief: Tin art film

## The idea
Every era of marketing, made of the things marketing was made of, each one forming the Tin mark. Then the last
form: a terminal, where your agent now runs it.

Tin is an open-source marketing system for coding agents: you connect it to Claude Code, Codex or Cursor and your
agent runs the marketing. The name is the anchor: pressed-tin advertising signs were one of the first mass
advertising media.

## Style references (in `refs/films/`)
- `fable-5.mp4` (Anthropic, 20 s, 1:1): the numeral 5 rebuilt from about 22 archival materials (butterfly plates,
  maps, eggs, flags, a cyanotype, an X-ray, a petri dish), about 0.8 s each, cut on the beat, no text, then a title
  card on a brand colour with a grid.
- `opus-5-5.mp4` (Anthropic, 20 s, 16:9): one horizon curve in about 25 textures, three words typed across the cuts
  ("There's more to discover"), then the wordmark.
Take their grammar (one persistent shape, many real materials, rhythm, restraint, quiet end card). Never their
content, shapes or type.

## The arc of materials (a starting list, not a script; replace anything weak)
Wood-type poster letters · pressed-tin advertising signs · trade cards · newspaper classifieds · seed-catalogue
plates · matchbook covers · postage stamps and labels · halftone dots · neon tubing · punch cards · billboard
paste-up · radio dial · early web banner pixels · a search results page · the terminal (terminal blue), where the
mark resolves.

## Words
Three or four words in total, typed across cuts. Candidates to judge, not to use blindly: "Every era had its
marketing." / "Now your agent runs it." Keep whatever is shortest and true.

## End card
The Tin mark on paper with a hairline grid, then `tin.computer`. Lime may appear here.

## Format
2160×2160, 24 fps, about 20 seconds. A 16:9 cut later.
