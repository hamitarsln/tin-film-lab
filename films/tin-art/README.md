# Tin art film

`out/tin-art.mp4`: 2160 × 2160, 24 fps, 21.6 s, mixed at −14 LUFS, and it loops. `out/tin-art-share.mp4` is the same
cut at a lighter bitrate. `out/poster.png` is a single frame.

Every era of marketing, made of the things marketing was made of, each one forming the Tin mark. The mark never moves;
the material changes under it on the beat, and the cuts get faster as history does: a pressed-tin ceiling, an 1843
playbill, trade cards, labels, want ads, a halftone, a tin sign, billboards and painted walls, matchbooks, a radio
dial, the roadside neon of the 1950s, punched cards. Everything stops on a 1996 banner GIF and a 2004 page of search
results ("Your agent"), then the drop lands the terminal: terminal blue, the agent's marketing log ("runs it."). The
blue drains to the ink mark on Tin's paper, `tin.computer` with the lime cursor, and the film cuts back to its first
frame.

## The track
**House Vibez** (Mixkit #745, Mixkit Stock Music Free License), song bars 25–35: 122 BPM, 11 bars, 21.64 s, the drop on
film bar 9 at 15.738 s. It is the one track in the library with a stop and a drop back to back: two beats of near
silence, a hit, a 16th fill, then the bass and drums back at full. Its breakdown also accelerates by itself — a hit per
bar, then an 8th-note pulse as the bass drains, then harder 8ths over a riser with no bass at all — so every step of the
picture's acceleration has a step in the music under it. Runners-up and the measurements behind the pick are in
`docs/MUSIC.md`.

## The edit
47 shots: 16 board stills, 27 catalogue materials flashed in the strobe (as Fable flashes about 55), the terminal and
the end card. Every cut is on the music's grid, snapped to the nearest frame (at most 21 ms off). The full cut list
with positions, treatments and holds is `docs/EDIT.md`, generated from the list the film renders.

| Film bars | Music | Cutting |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | a hit on each downbeat, the bass receding | every two beats (24 frames), each material pushed in 3% a second |
| 3–4 | the same, a riser beginning | every beat (12 frames) |
| 5–6 | an 8th-note pulse; the bass falls away | every 8th (6 frames) |
| 7 | no bass, the riser peaking, a hit on every 8th | every 16th (3 frames), the board stills on the strong 8ths |
| 8 | **the stop** | two holds, each on a hit: the banner GIF and the search page, "Your agent" |
| 9 | **the drop** | the terminal, its log scrolling a line per beat, "runs it." |
| 10–11 | the groove, fading | the blue drains in one beat; the grid card; `tin.computer` through the fade |

## How it is made
- `src/index.html` is the film. `window.seek(t)` paints the frame for time t from nothing but t (`src/film.js`: the
  cut list on the music grid of `music-cut/cue.js`, the push, the drift, the log, the drain, the words, grain seeded
  by the frame number). `node tools/determinism.mjs src/index.html …` confirms every frame is the same whatever was
  painted before it. Open the page in a browser and click to preview it with the music.
- `src/engine.js` holds the mark (`src/mark-fit.js`: the traced mark with the n's arches refitted as smooth curves by
  `scripts/markfit.py`), Tin's paper and grain, and the four treatments: A objects along the strokes, B material
  inside the mark, C material with the mark cut out as paper, D the mark built by the material.
- `src/stills.js` defines every material; `src/board.html` paints any of them as a still (`node scripts/board.mjs`).
- Materials are real public-domain or CC0 scans and photographs, each recorded with its rights field verbatim in
  `materials/ASSETS.md` (generated by `scripts/assets.py`). Only the banner, the search page, the terminal and the end
  card are made in code, and they are recorded as such.
- Render: `scripts/final.sh` (determinism check, 2160 render with `tools/render.mjs`, `tools/mix.sh`, the share copy,
  the poster and the review sheets). Animatics: `node tools/render.mjs video --page src/index.html --w 1080 --h 1080
  --fps 24 --sub 1`, then `tools/mix.sh`, `scripts/filmsheets.py` and `scripts/syncsheet.py`.

## What I would improve
- **Colour pressed tin.** The opening panel is a 1930s-plus HABS black-and-white photograph. It reads as stamped metal
  because the light rakes its ridges, but a colour photograph of a painted or bare tin ceiling, lit the same way, would
  open on the material's sheen and colour. The open-licence archives had none at 3000 px or more (`materials/ASSETS.md`,
  gaps); the Library of Congress rate limit stopped the last searches of Highsmith's colour work.
- **More object treatments.** Fable is about 40% objects along the outline; this film is 16% (trade cards, stamps,
  match safes, matchbooks). Punched cards, seed packets and tin cans as flat scans along the strokes would add more.
- **A truer 1996 and 2004.** The banner and the search page are made in code because no public-domain capture of a
  real 1996 banner or 2004 results page exists. They are honest about it, but they are the two least material frames
  in the film.
- **The 16:9 cut.** The layout is written against the frame size, but the mark's scale (77% of the width) and the
  knockout crops were chosen for a square; a 16:9 version needs its own crops and probably a smaller mark.
- **A bigger music cut.** House Vibez's stop is two beats long. A track whose silence lasted a full bar would let
  "Your agent" breathe longer before the drop.
