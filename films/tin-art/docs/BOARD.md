# Materials board: hand-off

The board is `out/board/BOARD.png`: 20 stills in arc order, 4 alternates and the end card, 5 across. Each still is a
2160 × 2160 PNG in `out/board/`; `seek(i)` in `src/index.html` paints still *i*. Scores are out of 60, from the
critique rubric (legibility, material truth, distinctness, craft, Tin's world, story). The three rounds are in
`docs/REVIEW_LOG.md`, sources and rights in `materials/ASSETS.md`, and how each file was made in `scripts/README.md`.
Treatments: A objects along the outline, B material inside the mark, C material with the mark cut out as paper,
D the mark built by the material itself (see `docs/REFERENCE.md`).

## What I would cut
Cut three to reach 17 materials and a tighter strobe:
1. **17 TV.** It is the second grey knockout after 12 and the weakest material in the broadcast run. The Lucky Motel's
   "TV" already nods to it.
2. **10 poster stamps.** The busiest still, and the fourth chain of small printed rectangles after 01, 03 and 13.
3. **05 biscuit tin.** Decorative rather than advertising, and 04 already carries "tin". Keep it only as a three-frame
   strobe beat.

Scores alone would cut 18, then 17 and 10. Keep 18 even though it scores lowest: it is the brief's web beat, and the film
needs it to reach the terminal. 05 (45) goes before 01, 08, 09 and 19 (44 each) because those are arc steps the brief
names, or the opening, and 05 is neither.

## Words
Recommended, 4 words: **"Your agent runs it."**
- Drop "Every era had its marketing": the 17–20 materials already say it.
- "it" points back to everything the viewer has just watched.
- Following Opus, the words enter whole on hard cuts, resting on the mark's top edge in Funktional Grotesk, ink on paper:
  - "Your agent" on the cut into 18, holding through 19.
  - "runs it." on the cut into 20 (the terminal).
  - Both holds lengthen to 0.4–1.2 s, like Fable's settle before its title.
- If 4 words feel long in the edit, use the 3-word fallback "Now, your agent." The terminal's own log spells out the
  verb.

## End card
Fable's two-card ending in Tin's colours (`docs/REFERENCE.md` section 6; still E on the board):
1. The terminal blue drains out of the mark and leaves it in ink (#080808) on Tin's paper, over a hairline ink grid.
   The grid is 9 % alpha with a pitch of about 150 px, aligned so the mark's cap line and baseline fall on grid lines.
   Hold about 1.5 s.
2. `tin.computer` in Funktional Grotesk Medium enters under the mark. A lime-cta block cursor sits after it, the only
   lime in the film. Hold about 2 s, then cut back to 01 so the film loops, as Fable does.

## For the human review
- **Rights:** every image is public domain, CC0 or "No known restrictions", recorded verbatim with its field in
  `materials/assets.json`. Anything marked "not evaluated" or "may be restricted" was skipped, and so was any Commons file
  with a hidden CC-BY licence. No people.
- **Content flag:** in 10, the Hohlwein Nymphenburg stamp has a small pink statue-like shape in front of the villa. It does
  not read as a person at board size; check it in motion.
- **Choices against the brief's list:**
  - "Postage stamps" became advertising poster stamps (Reklamemarken). Postage stamps are government issues, not
    advertising; poster stamps were posters printed as stamps.
  - Halftone has one real source, a c. 1905 halftone print on a Cooper Hewitt match safe, with its dots re-screened in
    code. Every other halftone print found was "rights status not evaluated" (`materials/ASSETS.md`).
  - Steps the brief didn't list but the archives supported: engraved shop cards, lithographed tins, can labels, painted
    walls and television.
- **Ground:** the six knockouts (02, 04, 07, 12, 14, 17) invert the figure. The mark is Tin's paper and the material fills
  the rest of the frame, as in Fable's stamp and city-plan frames. If every still should sit on paper, change `knock` to
  `inside` in `src/stills.js`; round 2 showed those materials read worse that way.
- **Brand flags:**
  - El Cortez neon was dropped because the hotel still operates.
  - One stamp in 10 advertises Lech-Elektrizitätswerke. Lechwerke AG still trades as LEW, but this is a 1910s Hohlwein
    design, not its logo.
  - 13 has a "Golden Guernsey" milk cover.
  - 04's lettering names Fabrik Seebach, a historic tinware factory.
- **Resolution:**
  - Three textures are upscaled slightly: 05 (1.24×), 06 (1.18×) and 15 (1.03×). Four Commons originals that were first
    throttled were fetched again at full size.
  - Stamps and some matchbooks are reduced Commons renditions (960–1920 px). They are drawn at 200–400 px, so this
    never shows.
- **Determinism:** `node tools/determinism.mjs src/index.html 1 2 … 25` reports all 25 stills identical whatever order
  they are painted in.
- **Re-render:** `node scripts/board.mjs`.
